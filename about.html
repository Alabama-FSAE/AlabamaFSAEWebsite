About

Two things worth knowing about us.

Formula SAE is an international collegiate engineering competition in which student teams design, build, and race a small formula-style car — from a completely blank sheet of paper. It isn't a race series. It's an engineering competition that happens to end with the cars on a track.

Each car is scored across two categories of events: static events, where judges — often practicing engineers from the automotive and motorsport industry — interrogate the team's design decisions, cost accounting, and business case directly; and dynamic events, where the car is actually driven on track.

Static Events

Design

A panel of judges reviews the car's engineering in detail and questions the team on every design decision — the single most heavily weighted event.

Cost & Manufacturing

Teams submit a full cost report for the car and defend their manufacturing and budgeting decisions.

Presentation

Teams pitch the car as a business case, as if presenting to a company's board deciding whether to put it into production.

Dynamic Events

Acceleration & Skidpad

Short, focused runs that isolate straight-line acceleration and pure cornering grip.

Autocross

A single timed lap of a tight, technical course — also used to set the field for Endurance.

Endurance & Efficiency

A long-distance race that tests reliability under sustained stress, scored alongside fuel/energy efficiency.

Teams from nearly 100 universities worldwide take part in Formula SAE each year, making it one of the largest and most competitive engineering design programs a student can join before graduating.
